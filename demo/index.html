01
Person A · data understanding & quality (METR-LA)

The raw signal

METR-LA holds 34,272 readings (every 5 minutes, 1 March – 27 June 2012) across 207 loop detectors in Los Angeles County, California. Before training any model, we locked the raw file's SHA-256 fingerprint and verified zero duplicate timestamps and zero gaps in the 5-minute clock grid.

The catch: 8.1% of cells are exact 0 (575,302 readings, including 97 blocks where every sensor reads 0 at once). Following the DCRNN benchmark, zeros are treated as sensor did not report — never as stopped traffic, never filled as targets, and never scored.

The curve shows the whole 207-sensor Los Angeles average by hour (stored clock): lowest at 17:00 evening rush (49.9) and highest at 22:00 (63.0).

02
Person B · features & leak-free split (Ashutosh)

One hour in, four answers out

Every sample reads the last 12 steps (1 hour) to forecast +5, +15, +30, and +60 minutes ahead. From those 12 steps, Phase 1 built 32 strictly local features per sensor — zero neighbour info allowed.

The chronological split uses the oldest 70% to train, next 10% to tune, and final 20% for a one-time test. An origin is kept only if all four future targets stay inside the same split — zero leakage across boundaries.

Inside the 70 / 10 / 20 time split & 12-step leak guard

Why we drop 12 timestamps at split boundaries and never shuffle rows.
Chronological timeline · Mar 1 – Jun 27, 2012 (34,272 five-minute steps) real

Adjacent 5-minute traffic speeds are nearly identical — random row splitting puts 09:00 in test and 09:05 in train, causing severe data leakage. Requiring all 4 horizons (t+1..t+12) inside one split drops the last 12 steps before each cut, guaranteeing zero target overlap.

Phase 1: every sensor on its own

Persistence predicts speed stays flat. Historical average uses that sensor's usual speed for that weekday and clock time (training split only).

Random Forest averages 100 independent trees; XGBoost adds boosted trees one-by-one on residuals. Adding 4 historical-average features created XGBoost + HA 36, the Phase 1 winner across all horizons.

Re-scored on 1.245M valid test targets, XGBoost + HA cuts 60-min error by 31% over Persistence (5.380 → 3.688) — yet errors grow sharply at 30–60 min because single sensors cannot see upstream or downstream traffic.

Is the win real? 2-day block bootstrap

Resample the 24 held-out Los Angeles test days 2,000 times to build 95% confidence intervals.
Paired 2-day block bootstrap real data
  1. Sum each model's absolute error per test day at +60 min (24 test days)
  2. Draw 12 circular 2-day blocks (with replacement) → 24 resampled days
  3. Compute the MAE gap on that resampled test period
  4. Repeat 2,000 times; take the middle 95% interval
  5. If the entire interval sits strictly above 0, improvement is not luck

2-day blocks preserve weekday autocorrelation. Browser draws differ slightly from the repo's NumPy seed 42 by ~0.01; official repo intervals are shown side-by-side.

The graph gap Phase 1 left open

A jam travels against the traffic

When a Los Angeles freeway slows downstream, congestion queues travel upstream toward incoming cars. A local sensor reading only its own last hour cannot see that jam arriving until speeds already drop.

Neighbouring sensors downstream saw it earlier. Phase 2 models pass messages across the METR-LA road-distance graph (`adj_mx.pkl`, 207 nodes, 1,722 directed edges) so every sensor sees surrounding conditions.

Phase 2: let the road graph talk

Same METR-LA file, same 70/10/20 split, same valid targets (persistence parity error = 0.000). Inputs form a [B, 12, 207, 7] tensor: z-scored speed, missing flag, hour sin/cos, weekday sin/cos, and weekend flag.

GAT (Member 2) uses masked multi-head attention so each sensor weights its neighbours dynamically. DCRNN (Member 3) runs K-hop forward and reverse random-walk diffusion inside a recurrent encoder–decoder.

To isolate the road graph cleanly, we tested 3 structural controls: GRU local (no graph), Identity (self-loops only), and Shuffled (same link counts per node, wrong road destinations).

Inside GAT (Graph Attention Network · Member 2)

Dense pure-PyTorch multi-head attention: scores real road neighbours, masks non-edges to −∞, and mixes messages.
Attention layer around LA sensor — real links toy jam simulation

Shows real METR-LA road neighbours from adj_mx.pkl and measured self-attention from seed 42. Click any neighbour node to simulate a sudden jam there and watch softmax attention shift dynamically (toy illustration).

  1. Shared GRU encodes each sensor's 12 steps [B, 12, 207, 7] → [B, 207, 64]
  2. Project & split into 2 heads of dim 32 [B, 2, 207, 32]
  3. Factored edge score LeakyReLU(a_src·Whᵢ + a_dst·Whⱼ) [B, 2, 207, 207]
  4. Mask non-edges (×) to -1e9, keep self-loops strictly 0 outside road graph
  5. Softmax + re-normalize rows to sum = 1.0 α sums to 1.0 per sensor
  6. Weighted sum of neighbour vectors + LayerNorm LayerNorm(h + Σ α·Whⱼ)
  7. Residual MLP head predicts +5/+15/+30/+60m Δ + last_speed → [B, 4, 207]
Measured attention budget (12 test origins, seed 42) real

Across all 207 sensors, GAT allocates ~9.7% weight to self and ~90.3% to neighbours (upstream/downstream overlap because most links run both ways). Note: attention weights are not causal proof of physical shockwaves.

Inside DCRNN (best model at 5–30 min · Member 3)

K-hop forward & reverse random-walk diffusion inside a 12→12 recurrent encoder–decoder over Los Angeles.
Diffusion footprint from LA sensor — real graph adj_mx.pkl
Hops K Direction
  1. Forward walk P_f = D_out⁻¹ A: information along road direction [207, 207] downstream transition
  2. Reverse walk P_r = D_in⁻¹ Aᵀ: queues backing up against flow [207, 207] upstream transition
  3. Expand 1..K hops: [I, P_f, P_r, P_f², P_r²] 2K + 1 = 5 spatial supports
  4. Replace linear gates inside GRU with DiffusionConv space + time fused at every step

Node positions are real METR-LA GPS coordinates across Los Angeles County; diffusion weights are computed live in-browser from adj_mx.pkl.

12 → 12 Encoder–Decoder unrolled architecture

Encoder consumes 12 past steps (`[B,12,207,7]`) into state `H [B,207,64]`; autoregressive decoder unrolls 12 future steps and extracts indices 1, 3, 6, 12 (+5, +15, +30, +60 min).

08
Scientific boundaries, Viva Q&A & reproduction

Honest limits & how to run it

  • Los Angeles dataset metadata. Speeds come in source units (likely mph) on the stored clock (timezone unverified); regions are coordinate bounding boxes, not freeway route names.
  • Single city & season. 4 months (Mar–Jun 2012) on one chronological split; bootstrap intervals are unadjusted for multiple comparisons.
  • Wall-clock & epoch caps. GAT finals stopped at the 60-epoch limit and DCRNN at its 50-minute cap (14 epochs on Apple M3 MPS), so both may be slightly under-trained.
  • Feature difference vs. XGBoost + HA. XGBoost + HA receives 4 target-time Historical Average lookup features that deep models do not; xgboost_32 vs gat/dcrnn is the feature-matched comparison.
  • Attention is not causal proof. GAT weights show what the model relied on statistically — not physical vehicle shockwave mechanics.
  • Software-only signal study. Arrivals follow an assumed Greenshields rule at one simulated approach, not live signal hardware.

Quick Viva Q&A Cheat Sheet — Click to Unfold

8 common cross-questions with short, defensible answers grounded in our repo outputs.
1. Why treat exact 0 as missing instead of a stopped traffic jam?

8.1% of cells (575,302 readings) are exact zeros, including 97 blocks where all 207 LA sensors drop to 0 simultaneously — recording failures. Following the upstream DCRNN METR-LA benchmark, we mask zeros out and never fill target zeros.

2. How do you prevent data leakage in inputs and splits?

Strict 70/10/20 chronological split; origins dropping the last 12 steps before boundaries so +60m targets never cross splits; causal forward-fill (past only) + training-only medians and global z-scaler (58.584 / 12.823).

3. Why did a "wrong" shuffled graph still beat GAT identity (+0.040)?

Because city-wide LA rush hours correlate across freeways, mixing even random sensors adds a small global cue (+0.040). Yet the true LA road graph beats shuffled by +0.149 [0.124, 0.174], proving ~79% of the gain comes from true road links. We reported H2 as contradicted honestly.

4. Why did DCRNN beat GAT (3.734 vs 4.175 at 60 min)?

GAT mixes 1-hop undirected neighbours once after GRU encoding (22,788 params), whereas DCRNN mixes K=2 hop forward & reverse diffusion inside every recurrent gate of both encoder and 12-step decoder (131,009 params).

5. Why did GAT lose to Phase 1 XGBoost + HA (3.688)?

XGBoost + HA gets 4 explicit target-time Historical Average features ("normal speed at that sensor, weekday, and hour") that deep models were not given. Against feature-matched XGBoost-32 (4.393), both GAT (4.175) and DCRNN (3.734) win clearly.

6. Do GAT attention weights prove traffic shockwaves?

No. Exported seed-42 weights (~90.3% on neighbours, ~9.7% on self) describe what the network relied on statistically, never physical fluid causality.

7. Why does forecast choice change signal delay by under 0.5 s/vehicle?

In congestion, switching from fixed timing (~2,179 s) to any adaptive controller (~27 s) captures virtually all the gain. Once green time responds to queue + approximate speed, even a perfect oracle forecast adds only +0.40 s/veh over XGBoost + HA.

8. How do you know Phase 1 and Phase 2 numbers are strictly comparable?

phase2_check_phase1_frozen.py checks zero edits since phase1-final, and Member 4's scorer passes a parity gate (max |ΔMAE| = 0.0) across the exact same 1,245,052 valid 5-min test targets.
